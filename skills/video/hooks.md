# Hook and retention

How the first seconds stop the scroll, and how the middle keeps the viewer to the payoff. Read it while writing the brief's concept and the storyboard's first and middle beats. The type files say which hook fits them; this file says how a hook and a hold are built.

## The first three seconds

A viewer decides in about a second, with the sound off and the platform's buttons over the frame.

| Time     | What is on screen                                                                                   |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Frame 0  | already composed: the subject and the hook's picture, no empty or black frame. It is also the cover |
| By 0.5 s | something changes: an object enters, a price is struck, a bubble arrives, the camera pushes         |
| By 1.0 s | the hook line reads (at most 7 words, in the safe zone)                                             |
| By 2-3 s | the promise or question is complete; a second change lands before 3 s (the "second hook")           |

- A voice-over starts at 0.0-0.3 s with the hook itself, never a greeting ("Halo semuanya", "Hai guys") or the brand's or channel's name.
- The logo and the brand's or channel's name wait for the ending. Opening on them tells the viewer it is an ad before giving a reason to stay.

## Hook types

Pick one per video, from the user's real material. Never fake one: a "before" that did not happen, a price that is not the price.

| Hook                  | The first frame shows                                   | Fits                               |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Result first          | the finished thing (the cake, the clean shoe), then how | promo, footage edit, explainer     |
| Price shock           | the price, or the strike-through, as the first picture  | promo, ad (when price is the hook) |
| Process close-up      | pouring, sizzling, cutting, packing an order            | menu, product, social content      |
| Question              | the "why" or "how come" with its picture                | visual story, explainer            |
| Warning               | "Jangan ... sebelum ..." with the thing in frame        | explainer, ad                      |
| Count                 | "3 hal ..." with the first item already arriving        | tips, social content               |
| Conflict              | the problem in one picture (a cracked phone, a WA chat) | chat story, ad                     |
| Hand or face in frame | a person using the product                              | footage edit, UGC-style ad         |
| Before and after      | the "before", with the turn coming                      | social content, promo              |

Indonesian hook lines that work as patterns, never copied as slogans: "Kalian wajib tahu ...", "Ternyata ...", "Jujur, ...", "Ini alasan ...", "Stop ...", "POV: ...", "Spill ...", "Yang ... wajib nonton". Fill them with this business's own fact, in its own register (`platforms.md`, "Words").

## Hold to the payoff

- **Open a loop, close it late.** The hook plants a question; the answer lands at about 70-85% of the length, and the call to action comes after it, never before. Showing the answer in the hook ends the reason to watch, unless the answer is the hook (price shock).
- **One re-hook** in a video over about 15 s, near the middle: a line or a picture that raises the stakes ("tapi yang ketiga ini paling penting", the hidden detail turned toward camera). In the storyboard it is a second `hook` beat in the middle, so the recipe gives it a hook's energy.
- **A change every 1.5-3 s:** a new shot, scale, element or colour. A change is cheap (the camera pushes); an event carries meaning (the price lands). Keep events coming, use changes between them; neither breaks the guide's reading time.
- **Lists:** promise the count up front, keep it visible as a progress cue, and put the strongest item last or second to last.
- **Length follows the idea.** A single-idea video stops when the idea is done; padding to fill time is where viewers leave. A story or a lesson earns more time than a promo. The recipe sets the tempo (`brief.md`).

## Reading the numbers after posting

When `get_social_insights` gives watch time or retention for a posted video, name the beat at the drop: a fall in the first 2 s means the hook; a slow slide through the middle means no re-hook or too long a beat; viewers leaving just before the ending means the payoff came too early. Carry that one finding into the next brief, not a rewrite of everything.
